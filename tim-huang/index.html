01 / ABOUT

You don't have to wait until
everything falls apart.

I'm Dr. Tim Huang, a Licensed Clinical Social Worker and Board-Approved Supervisor with a Doctorate in Social Work from Tulane University. I provide bilingual, culturally responsive therapy for adults seeking greater clarity, balance, and emotional well-being.

Many of my clients appear successful on the outside but privately struggle with burnout, perfectionism, anxiety, self-doubt, relationship challenges, or the emotional weight of constantly meeting others' expectations.

“Therapy isn't about becoming someone different—it's about creating a life that feels more authentic, connected, and aligned with what matters most to you.”

05 / GETTING TO KNOW TIM

A little more about
how I think about therapy.

What led you to become a therapist?+

I've always been curious about how people make meaning of difficult experiences and continue moving forward despite adversity. As a social worker, I view mental health not only through an individual lens, but also within the context of culture, relationships, systems, and lived experiences.

How does your cultural background influence your work?+

As a bilingual therapist who has lived and worked across different cultural environments, I understand that many struggles cannot be separated from culture, family expectations, immigration experiences, identity, and belonging. I approach each person's story with curiosity and humility.

What surprises clients about your therapy style?+

My approach sits between purely supportive and highly directive. I strive to be warm and compassionate while also helping clients identify patterns, explore difficult questions, and practice new ways of responding to challenges.

How do you define success in therapy?+

Success looks different for every person. I don't measure it by whether life becomes easy. I look at whether clients feel more capable of responding to life in ways that are meaningful to them.

What conversations do you especially enjoy?+

I enjoy conversations about meaning, identity, relationships, culture, and personal growth—questions such as: Who am I beyond my responsibilities? What truly matters to me? How do I want to live, not just survive?
